Chapter 1.2

What the Margin
Actually Buys

A seventy percent margin, built in factories it doesn't own.

Where the value chain is owned, and where it is rented.

Pop Mart's economics look like a luxury brand's and its balance sheet looks like a publisher's. The company controls the two ends of the chain — the intellectual property at the front and the retail relationship at the back — and rents almost everything in between. That arrangement is what produces the margin. It is also what the margin is secured against.

Asset-light manufacturing is a deliberate choice, not an oversight. It converts fixed cost into variable cost and lets output scale without capital expenditure — the capability that made 1.1's margin expansion possible.

Three things lifted the margin. None of them are the product.

Management attributes the 2025 gross margin improvement to a higher overseas revenue mix, to a flexible supply-chain strategy that strengthened bargaining power with suppliers, and to concentrating procurement into core vendors to reduce unit cost. The company does not disclose how the 5.3 points split between them — but the character of all three is the same. Every driver is a sourcing or mix decision. None is a change in what is actually being sold.

Gross margin  ·  2022–2025

The two series move together almost exactly. Overseas revenue carries a higher realised price and a different channel mix, so the geographic shift is doing a large share of the work that gets described as operational efficiency.

The bill arrives below the gross line.

A rented supply chain does not remove cost — it relocates it. The savings show up in cost of goods, where they lift gross margin. The exposure shows up in operating expense, where it does not.

Logistics cost grew roughly 1.5 times faster than revenue. At current scale that gap is comfortably absorbed — gross profit expanded by more than RMB 18bn in the same year. The question is not whether it is affordable now. It is what happens to a rented chain when the two variables that made it cheap — concentrated volume and favourable freight — move the other way.

Supplier concentration is presented as a bargaining advantage, and at current volumes it is. The same structure describes a dependency: the party that can negotiate best is also the party with the fewest alternatives if a core vendor is disrupted.

So what
The margin is owned. The means of production are rented.

This is not a criticism — it is the design. Asset-light manufacturing is precisely what let the company multiply output in 2025 without a capital cycle, and it is the mechanism behind the margin expansion in 1.1. The trade is real capacity control in exchange for real operating leverage.

What it means is that the durable asset is upstream, not downstream. The moulds can be re-tendered; the freight can be re-contracted; the stores can be re-sited. The only thing in the chain that cannot be replaced is the IP itself — which makes the concentration of revenue in a single character the structural question, not a marketing one. That is 1.3.
