Chapter 2.4

One Product,
Three Businesses

Running them on a single playbook is the quiet risk.

Not three geographies. Three stages of the same sequence.

The obvious way to segment this company is by map. The more useful way is by how far each market has actually travelled through the sequence this section is named after. Arrive is now proven everywhere. Belong is proven in one place and plausible in another. Repeat is proven in one place only.

Sort the four reported regions on that basis and they collapse into three businesses with genuinely different economics, different risks, and different correct metrics.

The Americas and Europe are grouped not because they are similar to each other — one is 64% online, the other 36% — but because they occupy the same position in the sequence. Both have demonstrated arrival at extraordinary speed and neither has yet demonstrated anything after it.

Belong is marked inferred for Asia-Pacific because the evidence is indirect but real: the highest offline revenue per store in the group, at roughly RMB 55.6m, is difficult to produce from single visits alone. It is an inference from store productivity, not a disclosed cohort metric, and it should be labelled as such.

The 2026 target needs the unproven business to keep working.

Management has guided to group revenue growth of not less than 20% in 2026 — a floor of roughly RMB 44.5bn. Set a growth rate for each of the three businesses and see whether the group clears it.

Try holding The Experiment flat at zero. On any reasonable assumption for the other two, the group misses its own floor — which is the structural point. The guidance is not a statement about China.

So what
The group reports one growth rate. It is running three companies at three different ages.

A single consolidated number is exactly the wrong instrument here. It blends a mature franchise growing on depth, an adjacent extension growing on siting, and a two-year-old acquisition engine growing on novelty — then presents the average as though it described a strategy.

The practical consequence is in the operating decisions, not the disclosure. One store target across four networks with four different jobs. One margin benchmark across channels with entirely different cost structures. One growth expectation applied to a market where the customer relationship is a decade old and to one where it is eighteen months old. Each of those is a small mistake that only becomes visible after it has been made at scale.

And beneath the operating question sits the one this section cannot answer. Every market has now arrived. Whether they belong — whether the object means the same thing in Bangkok, in Los Angeles, in Copenhagen — is not in any disclosure, and may not be knowable for another two years. What can be said is that the company is being valued as though the answer is already in.

Everyone arrived. Almost no one has been asked to come back yet.
