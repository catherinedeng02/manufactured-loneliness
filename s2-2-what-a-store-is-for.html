Chapter 2.2

What a Store
Is Actually For

One store in Asia-Pacific earns roughly what two in China do.

The obvious metric gets the ranking wrong.

Divide regional revenue by regional store count and the Americas looks like the most productive network in the group. It is also the least accurate thing you can say about it, because 64% of Americas revenue never went through a store at all. Strip the online channel out and the ranking inverts.

A further caveat cuts the same way. China's offline revenue is spread across 445 stores and 2,396 robot shops, but is attributed here to stores alone. The true per-store figure for China is therefore lower than shown — which widens the gap rather than narrowing it.

The same shop is doing four different jobs.

Productivity gaps of this size usually mean the asset is not comparable across markets. That is the case here. A Pop Mart store has a different function in each region, and each function should be underwritten against a different return.

China
Coverage
445 stores · 2,396 robot shops
A dense network built to be near everyone. Individual productivity is low by design; the robot shops exist precisely to add reach at minimal fixed cost. This network is optimised for frequency, not for footfall value.
Asia-Pacific
Conversion
85 stores · travel retail
Sited at tourist destinations and airports, where the buyer is already in a purchasing frame of mind and looking for something to carry home. The highest offline productivity in the group, and the closest thing to a repeatable overseas model.
The Americas
Showroom
64 stores · 64% of revenue online
The store validates a brand that most customers meet on a phone. Its job is partly to make the online purchase feel legitimate — a function that generates revenue somewhere other than the till it sits behind.
Europe
Media
36 stores · landmark sites
Cambridge, Copenhagen, Rotterdam, Lyon, Barcelona. Positional real estate bought for visibility. Judged on revenue per square metre this is the weakest network in the group; judged as a brand-building line item it may be the cheapest.

Two of these four jobs produce revenue directly. The other two produce it somewhere else on the P&L, or not yet at all — which is why a single store-opening target is a poor way to steer this business.

Two networks that look alike on a slide.

At the end of 2025 the group operated 630 stores across twenty countries and 2,637 robot shops. Nearly every robot shop is in China. The overseas footprint is almost entirely full stores — a structurally more expensive, higher-productivity, and considerably less flexible network.

So what
A store is not distribution here. It is whichever scarce thing the market is missing.

In China the scarce thing was proximity, so the network optimised for reach and accepted low unit productivity. In Asia-Pacific the scarce thing is the moment of intent, so the network sits in airports and tourist districts and earns roughly twice per site. In the Americas the scarce thing is legitimacy for a brand discovered through a feed. In Europe it is simply awareness.

Managing all four under a single store-opening target and a single revenue-per-store benchmark is how a business ends up expanding the wrong format in the wrong market. The 2025 numbers do not show that happening. They do show four networks being built at once, with only one of them fully understood.

Which leaves the harder question untouched. Store productivity measures how well a market converts. It says nothing about whether anyone comes back. For China that answer is disclosed in detail. For every other market it is not disclosed at all — which is 2.3.
