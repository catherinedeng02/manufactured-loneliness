Chapter 2.1

Three Doors Into
the Same Room

The Americas found it online. Asia found it on a street.

Four regions. Four different businesses.

FY2025 was the first year Pop Mart reported on a four-region basis, and the disclosure makes something visible that group-level growth had been hiding. Every region grew in triple digits. None of them grew the same way. The difference is not pace — it is the door the customer walked through.

One market is not like the others.

Placed side by side, three regions cluster and one separates. China, Asia-Pacific and Europe all run majority offline, in a band between roughly a third and two-fifths online. The Americas sits alone at 64% online — and it is simultaneously the fastest-growing region in the group, at 748%.

Online share of regional revenue  ·  FY2025
Within the Americas, revenue from Pop Mart's own app and website grew 1,395% and TikTok revenue grew 866%. In the first half of 2025 the app briefly reached number one on the US App Store shopping chart. This is not a retail rollout that happens to have an e-commerce arm. It is a digital acquisition engine with sixty-four stores attached.

Why the door changes the business.

Channel mix is usually treated as an operational detail. Here it determines four separate things — and each of them compounds.

Consequence 01
Who owns
the customer
An app purchase produces an identity, a history and a push channel. A store purchase produces a transaction. China closed this gap with 72.6m registered members; a new market has no such asset on day one.
Consequence 02
Where demand
actually comes from
Offline demand is generated by the location. Online demand is generated by a feed. One is a lease renewal; the other is an algorithm the company does not control.
Consequence 03
What the
purchase feels like
The blind box is a physical ritual — the shake, the queue, the reveal in front of others. Shipped to a doorstep four days later, the same object delivers a materially thinner version of the same experience.
Consequence 04
How durable
the demand is
Feed-driven acquisition is fast, cheap and reversible. It builds a customer base at speed without necessarily building a habit — which is the question 2.3 puts to the disclosures, and finds unanswered.
So what
The fastest-growing market is also the least like the one the playbook was written for.

Fifteen years of operating experience in China produced a specific competence: run a dense physical network, convert footfall into membership, and monetise a ritual that happens in a shop. The Americas grew 748% in 2025 without using most of that competence. Two-thirds of the revenue arrived through a channel where the queue, the shelf and the shake do not exist.

That is not a failure — it is arguably the most impressive commercial result in the company's history. But it means the group's largest source of growth is being produced by a machine it has been running for roughly two years, in a market where the underlying behaviour has not yet had time to prove itself repeatable.

Which raises the obvious question about the other channel. If the store is where the ritual lives, what is a store actually worth — and why do eighty-five of them in Asia out-earn four hundred and forty-five in China? That is 2.2.
